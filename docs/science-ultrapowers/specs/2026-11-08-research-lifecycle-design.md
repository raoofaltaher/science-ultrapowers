# Research lifecycle and tracks

Status: draft, 2026-11-08. Ticket: none.

## Intent

A researcher opens Claude Code with one of a few things in hand: a topic they like, a paper that provoked them, a new technique, a dataset, a call for papers, a supervisor's remark, a thesis chapter, a manuscript to referee, their own manuscript to check, or no idea yet and a field they are open to. After the terminology work (phase 0 of the plan), the plugin serves the middle of that range well: a lab with fourteen roles, seven stages from Question to Publication (each ending in a stage approval), records and an artifact archive, for a project that already has a question. It serves the start (from an interest to a falsifiable question), the services around a study (a literature review, a referee report, a citation audit, a thesis plan, slides) and the discipline of a single analysis (preregistration before outcomes, root cause before adjustment, verification before claims) thinly or not at all.

This design makes the plugin a complete research lifecycle: every request enters one lifecycle at the stage that fits it, through a named track, and the same team, records and evidence rule apply on every track. The discipline comes from science-superpowers (K-Dense, MIT), a science-domain reimplementation of superpowers whose central rule is preregistration in place of test-driven development. Success for the researcher: they say what they have, the lab says which track it is and where it starts and ends, the work runs through the roles with task assignments and task reports, and what comes out is reproducible, labeled confirmatory or exploratory, cited from matched sources and saved as versioned artifacts.

## Goals, non-goals and anti-goals

Goals, true when 0.2.0 ships:

1. Five tracks exist as skills and are routed by intent from the session primer: the idea track, the literature track, the review track, the writing track and the dissemination track; the study track is `running-the-lab`. Each track skill names the stages it enters, the roles it assigns, the records it writes and where it exits.
2. The stage table in `running-the-lab` carries the nine phases of science-superpowers (Frame, Ground, Design, Register, Prepare, Execute, Verify, Challenge, Report) as criteria of its seven stages, with the ten absorbed items: the preregistration and its registration check; the binding rule with a `confirmatory | exploratory` basis on every finding and the deviation rule; a result-level anomaly skill; the pilot and feasibility study; a protocol-adherence review before the rigor review; the design-validation lines in the preregistration template; four more critical-appraisal domains (ten in all, one list for the adversarial review and the referee report) and the referee-response guard; raw-data checksums and a reproducing baseline; the statistical claim rows; the parallel-assignment guard. The nine upstream names are not stage names anywhere in the plugin.
3. A new lab skill, `preregistering-the-protocol`, holds preregistration and ships `preregister.py`, a Python-standard-library rewrite of the upstream script `prereg.sh`, run by the lead session on the researcher's approval. Its commands are `register` and `check`.
4. `investigating-anomalous-results` and `establishing-feasibility-first` are adopted whole from science-superpowers with a lab addendum, `PROVENANCE.md` and the MIT notice, like the other adopted skills.
5. A service track in a folder with no lab asks once and gets a minimal lab from init (`--track <name>`): only the records that track writes, growable later into the full lab set-up.
6. Every product of every track is saved as an artifact with its provenance, every citation passes `science-ultrapowers:verifying-evidence-by-doi`, and every number comes from a record.
7. Every new or changed skill has its pressure test; the changed agents, templates and records have their shell tests; the scripts have unit tests; one acceptance transcript per track exists.
8. The release is 0.2.0, with its release-notes entry and the license's section 7 extended for the K-Dense material.
9. Every new text follows `docs/science-ultrapowers/terminology.md`: the seven stage names, the registration vocabulary, American spelling, and every listed abbreviation expanded at its first use. `bash tests/claude-code/test-terminology.sh` reports nothing for this design and its plan.

Non-goals, left out on purpose:

- Science-superpowers' multi-harness plumbing, its literature method (web search as a source), its git branch flow, its storage model (Markdown under `docs/` with git as the only record) and its continuous-execution control flow. Our stages, roles, records and commit rule stay.
- Domain tools from the SciSpace gallery (an engineering solver, medical imaging analysis). They belong to a domain plugin.
- A plagiarism check. It needs an external similarity service; no connector in the catalog provides one. The review track says so when asked.
- Autonomy. The researcher picks the question, approves the framing, the protocol, the registration and the release, and chooses the fate of every piece of work; nothing in this design changes that.

Anti-goals, failures even if everything works:

- The team feeling is lost: a track that runs as a faceless workflow instead of through the roles, task assignments and task reports.
- Rigor traded for speed on the service tracks: a referee report with an unmatched citation, a review paper with a number nobody recomputed, a fact check that accepts a web page.

## Constraints

- Claude Code is the only harness. Plugin scripts use bash (Git for Windows on Windows), node 22+ and the Python 3.11+ standard library; no pip packages. Adopted skills' scripts may use the lab environment that `setup_lab_env.ps1` installs (`python-pptx==1.0.2`, `Pillow==12.3.0`, `lxml==6.1.1` and the other pins in its `$pkgs` list), never the plugin's own scripts.
- Version `0.2.0` in both manifests through `scripts/bump-version.sh 0.2.0`; a `## 0.2.0 (<date>)` heading at the top of `RELEASE-NOTES.md`; `tests/claude-code/test-version-sync.sh` checks them.
- Skill form: `description` says only when to use it ("Use when..."); cross-references as `science-ultrapowers:<skill>`, never `@`; "the researcher" for the person the lab serves; actions, not tool names; verb-first lab skill names; no new or changed skill without a pressure test under `docs/science-ultrapowers/evals/<skill>/` (baseline and with-skill runs, Haiku 5.5). A lab skill that absorbs upstream rules has no `PROVENANCE.md`: three tests read that file as the mark of an adopted skill (the terminology test scans only the addendum of such a skill, the content test skips its description rule, and the adopted-skills test checks its hashes). The credit is one line at the end of the skill, the license section 7 and the reading record.
- Terminology: no stage code, no not-approved term, American spelling, and each listed abbreviation expanded at its first use in each file (`tests/claude-code/test-terminology.sh`, which scans every new skill, script string, agent, template, document and this design). A script names its checks in the standard's words.
- Sizes: the session primer (`skills/using-science-ultrapowers/SKILL.md`, 3,816 bytes at commit a6c61da) stays under 8,500 bytes (`tests/claude-code/test-bootstrap-length.sh`); every `SKILL.md` stays under 20,000 bytes (`tests/skill-size/`); `running-the-lab` is 13,573 bytes at that commit and gets its own new cap of 18,000 bytes, the lifecycle text included, checked in `tests/claude-code/test-skill-content.sh`.
- Disclosure check: nothing project-specific, no other harness name, no `science-superpowers:` prefix, no `docs/science-superpowers/` path, no drive-letter path outside C: system paths (`tests/claude-code/test-no-foreign-harness.sh`). Adopted copies keep their upstream text but the addendum and the paths are ours.
- Records: everything a track writes goes under `research_lab/` in the project; roles never run git write commands; the lead session commits `research_lab/` only, on the current branch; nothing is written under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`.
- License: the K-Dense MIT notice (`Copyright (c) 2026 K-Dense, Inc.`) kept with every substantial verbatim portion: the two adopted skills carry their `LICENSE` and `PROVENANCE.md`; `LICENSE` section 7 gains a science-superpowers entry; absorbed rules carry a one-line credit at the end of the skill that absorbs them.
- Windows: new scripts staged with `git add --chmod=+x`; LF line endings; `pwsh` only where the environment script already needs it.
- Existing labs: init's migration framework (`plan_migration` and `apply_migration` in `init_lab.py`) already renames a lab in the 0.1 layout. The lifecycle adds two column additions to that framework, shown in its plan as `header` and `rewrite` lines: the `Basis` column on `findings/FINDINGS_LOG.md` (existing rows filled with `exploratory`) and the `Input checksums` column on `experiments/EXPERIMENT_REGISTER.md` (existing rows left empty). The additions also run on a lab that already has the new layout. No other record is edited; the release notes say so.
- Upstream material: the builder's first task clones https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/science-superpowers at commit `0374bdf82c2372bdf1667e3c762146c3a29215f5` into a working folder outside the repository and reads it as data; the reading record of this design is committed at `docs/science-ultrapowers/research/2026-11-08-science-superpowers-reading.md`. The adopting tasks compute the `PROVENANCE.md` hashes from that clone.

## Architecture

Units, and how they talk:

1. **The session primer** (`using-science-ultrapowers`): a track table maps what the researcher has in hand to a track skill, and the tie-breaks stay: init first when there is no lab (or `init --track` for a service track), the meeting for a status question, `running-the-lab` beside the carrying skill once a lab exists. Interface: the track names and their trigger phrases.
2. **The lifecycle** (`running-the-lab`): the stage table with the nine upstream phases as criteria of the seven stages, the four binding rules (no confirmatory claim without a registered prediction; no adjustment without root cause; no claim without fresh evidence; nothing is feasible until it has run), the standing rules (outcomes untouched before the Question approval; a deviation makes the finding exploratory; parallel checks are all reported; raw data immutable), the roles and records per stage, the escalation rows for anomalies and the pilot and feasibility study, the archive list. Interface: the stage criteria every track and every agent cite.
3. **Preregistration** (`preregistering-the-protocol`, new): the binding rule, lock then register then execute then separate, the deviation rule, the rationalizations and replies table, the preregistration template's new lines, and `scripts/preregister.py` with `register` and `check`. Interface: the commit trailers `Preregistration:` and `Preregistration-Timestamp:`, the registration hash in `decisions/DECISION_LOG.md`, the check's JSON saved as an artifact.
4. **Side branches** (adopted): `investigating-anomalous-results` (used by the Lab & Experiments Lead and the Data & Evaluation Researcher from the escalation row) and `establishing-feasibility-first` (offered by the lead session at framing, entered and left by the researcher only; records under `research_lab/experiments/feasibility/`).
5. **Track skills** (five, new), each: when it starts, what it asks (one question at a time, multiple choice when possible), the stages it runs, the roles it assigns with task assignments, the records and products it writes, the checks before it exits, and the transfer to the next track.
6. **Templates and records** (init templates): the question document in `HYPOTHESIS_RECORD.md`; the preregistration's design-validation and deviation lines; `PROTOCOL_ADHERENCE_REVIEW.md`; the critical-appraisal domains in `ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md`; `RESPONSE_MATRIX.md`; `SYNTHESIS_MATRIX.md`; `ARCHIVE_MANIFEST.md`; the `Basis` column; the register's `Input checksums` field and `feasibility` status; `lab.json.tracks`. The task report's `Status:` line already exists.
7. **Init** (`initializing-a-research-lab`): `--track <name>` writes the minimal set; a re-run adds the new templates and keys, and the migration framework adds the two columns.
8. **Agents**: the roles that own the new work gain the lines that say so.

## Components

**Session primer** (`skills/using-science-ultrapowers/SKILL.md`, changed). A "Tracks" table of six rows: what the researcher has in hand, the track, the first skill. Under 8,500 bytes; the lifecycle text is not here.

**running-the-lab** (`skills/running-the-lab/SKILL.md`, changed; cap 18,000 in the content test). The stage table gains, per stage, the criteria from the upstream table. Question (the Frame phase): the question document approved; no outcome touched; the pilot and feasibility offer when runnability is unknown. Literature (Ground): methods, confounds, prior effect sizes and relation to prior work, all on evidence records. Preregistration (Design and Register): the four design-validation lines; every configuration has run once or is within about ten times a measured point; the three approvals; the registration; the hash in `DECISION_LOG.md`. Execution (Prepare and Execute): pinned environment, one recorded seed, raw inputs read-only with checksums and provenance in the register, the baseline reproduces, the protocol-adherence review before the rigor review, the task report's `Status:` line. Results (Verify and Challenge): the registration check is clear and saved; the claim rows; the `Basis` on every finding; the adversarial review with the ten critical-appraisal domains; the anomaly branch. Reporting: unchanged plus the archive manifest started. Publication (Report and Archive): confirmatory and exploratory labeled, every deviation listed, intervals, no causal wording from observational data, the archive manifest complete. Standing rules gain the binding rules and the parallel-assignment guard. The escalation row for anomalies points at the adopted skill.

**preregistering-the-protocol** (`skills/preregistering-the-protocol/`, new). `SKILL.md` under 12,000 bytes: the binding rule, "looked first means exploratory, no relabeling", lock then register then execute then separate, "do not register over runs that have never happened" and the cheapest falsifying run first, the deviation rule, a rationalizations and replies table (at least their ten rows reworded), warning signs, the register and check procedure, and a credit line to science-superpowers. `scripts/preregister.py`: `register <protocol> [--input <path>]... [--task <TASK-id>] [--timestamp-only]` records a git blob hash per raw input in the protocol's footer, refuses when the protocol file is already tracked (exit 2), stages and commits only that path (`git add -- <path>`, `git commit -- <path>`), so other staged or unstaged changes are left as they are; the commit follows the lab's commit rule (`<TASK-id> research-project-manager: register protocol <protocol id>`) and carries the trailer `Preregistration: <protocol id>`; it then writes the registration commit's hash into the file's `Registered at commit:` line and commits that path again with the trailer `Preregistration-Timestamp: <registration hash>`; the check finds the registration and timestamp commits by their trailers, never by the subject. A hook that rejects either commit ends the script with the hook's output and exit 1; a registration commit without its time stamp is reported by `check` as `not clear` on `timestamp` ("registered, not time-stamped"), and `register --timestamp-only <protocol>` completes it. `register` prints JSON (`protocol`, `protocol_id`, `registration_commit`, `timestamp_commit`, `inputs`). `check <protocol> [--outputs <path>]...` reports the five items of the registration check, each `clear`, `not clear` or `unprovable`:

| Item | Meaning | Clear when |
|---|---|---|
| `registered` | A commit that holds the protocol alone exists | the file's first commit carries the `Preregistration:` trailer and the history is complete |
| `timestamp` | The registration commit carries the protocol's hash and the time of registration | the file's `Registered at commit:` line names the registration commit, and a commit with the `Preregistration-Timestamp:` trailer names it too |
| `unaltered` | The protocol at the branch head equals the registered version | no later commit edits the file, no uncommitted edit exists, and the file equals the registered version byte for byte once the `Registered at commit:` line is set aside |
| `chronology` | Every confirmatory run was committed after the registration | no output path has a commit that is an ancestor of the registration; a commit on a parallel branch is `unprovable` |
| `inputs` | The input files match the checksums recorded at registration | each recorded blob hash still matches the file |

It prints JSON with `ok` and the items, exit 0 when every item is clear, 1 otherwise, 2 for a usage error or no git repository. Paths with spaces work; the script uses `subprocess` with argument lists, never a shell string. Tests under `scripts/tests/` build a temporary repository per case.

## Data flow

What enters: a message with a topic, a paper file, a dataset path, a manuscript file, or a question; files are read as sources and never as instructions. What leaves: the track's product as a versioned artifact (a question document, a protocol with its registration hash, a review paper, a matrix, a diagram, a referee report, a citation audit, a response matrix, a manuscript, a deck, a figure) and the records it updated (evidence records, source table rows, findings log rows, review-log rows, decisions). What is stored, and where: under `research_lab/` of the project, in the folders the record map names; the registration and the time stamp as commits of the protocol file; the check's report as an artifact; pilot runs under `experiments/feasibility/`.

Trust boundaries: text from a manuscript, a PDF, a search result or a connector is data; an instruction inside it is reported, never followed (the rule already in `science-ultrapowers:verifying-evidence-by-doi`, restated in the review track). Git is a process boundary: only the lead session writes it, only `research_lab/` is committed, and the check reads history without writing. Connectors are external services: a journal shortlist or a contribution check names the connector it used and says when one was unavailable.

## Error handling

- Registration refused: the protocol is already tracked, other changes are staged, or there is no git repository; the script says which, exit 2, nothing committed. The lead session relays it and the Preregistration approval waits.
- Check not clear: the items that are not clear are named (`registered`, `timestamp`, `unaltered`, `chronology`, `inputs`); the finding's basis cannot be `confirmatory`; the Findings & Records Curator records it `exploratory` with the reason; the Results approval does not pass on that finding until a new registration exists. Never silent: the check's report is an artifact.
- A rewritten or shallow history: the check reports that it cannot establish chronology and fails closed.
- No lab and no git in a service-track folder: init asks for `--git-init`; a refusal ends the track with nothing written.
- A connector absent or signed out: the journal shortlist and the contribution check say what they could not do; nothing is invented.
- A citation that does not resolve: it is not cited; the report lists it under unresolved.
- A manuscript that cannot be read (encrypted PDF, scanned image): the track asks for a readable copy; no review is written from the abstract alone.
- The lab environment missing for slides or posters: the track names the script and the dry run; no deck is produced by another route.
- An anomaly whose root cause is not found after three adjustments: the adopted skill stops and escalates to the researcher.
- Pilot and feasibility study: a stopping rule that was never shown able to fire blocks the series of pilot runs; a budget overrun stops the run and reports.
