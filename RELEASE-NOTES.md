# Release notes

## 0.2.2 (2026-11-09)

Two more init defects from the first upgrade of a real lab (issues 2 and 3).

- A project that tracks its `references/` folder in git keeps it tracked: init never adds the ignore line for it, and the plan carries a warning that says why. Before, every run added the line back, and new full texts were silently ignored.
- The migration re-points the archive's records: a `link:` target or a file name in `artifacts/versions.jsonl` and `artifacts/artifacts.jsonl` that names a renamed folder or file follows the rename, shown as `rewrite ... paths` lines. Before, `artifacts.py` reported such links as missing after the migration.

## 0.2.1 (2026-11-09)

A patch for init's migration, found on the first upgrade of a real 0.1 lab (issue 1).

- A record kept in sections (rows, a note, more rows) is one table: the migration rewrites every row whose cell count is the header's, to the end of the file. Before, a review log with a resolution note after its first rows had only those rows rewritten, and the plan showed nothing.
- The 0.1 finding types `code-audit`, `data-audit` and `doc-audit` become `code inspection`, `data inspection` and `document inspection`, as the standard says. <!-- legacy -->
- A cell that starts with a 0.1 status word and holds more text (for example a priority with a note after it) is left as it is and listed in the plan's warnings, so the researcher can edit it by hand.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-08)

The terminology release and the research lifecycle release. A lab in the 0.1 layout migrates through `science-ultrapowers:initializing-a-research-lab`; read the breaking changes below first.

- The terminology standard: `docs/science-ultrapowers/terminology.md` fixes the plugin's terms and writing rules, with one meaning for each term and a source for each term. `tests/claude-code/test-terminology.sh` enforces it: it refuses a not-approved term, a stage code, a British spelling and an abbreviation that is not expanded at its first use, in the lab skills, the addenda of the adopted skills, the agents, the templates, the documents, the hooks and the scripts' messages.
- The rename: the lab's folders, files, record ids, status words and agent ids carry the standard's names, with readable id prefixes (for example `FND-` finding, `EXP-` experiment, `REV-` review, `REQ-` request, `DEC-` decision and `TASK-` task, from the twelve in `idPrefixes`). The ten numbered folders and the files in them became `project`, `literature`, `findings`, `experiments`, `reviews`, `requests`, `decisions`, `planning`, `automation` and `tool_vetting`. Seven agent ids changed to match their titles, and each of the fourteen agents names its roles in the Contributor Roles Taxonomy (CRediT).
- Init's migration of a lab in the 0.1 layout: running `science-ultrapowers:initializing-a-research-lab` shows a plan first, then renames the folders and files (with `git mv` when the files are tracked), rewrites the headers and status words and the `lab.json` keys, and changes no free-text cell. A second run plans nothing. Every other script exits 2 with the migration message on a lab in the old layout.
- The scholarly output style: `output-styles/scholarly-explanatory.md`, installed by init to `.claude/output-styles/`, makes the lead session write plain scholarly English, match each claim to the design and take every number from the findings log or the key results table.
- The scripts' new verbs and words, with the old forms kept as hidden aliases for one release: `artifacts.py provenance` (was `lineage`), `findings add | list | review` (was `check`), `respond` (was `mark-addressed`), `save --task` (was `--command`), `verify_doi.py` status `matched` (was `VERIFIED`) and `--sources-bib` (was `--registry-bib`), `jev_ask.py --rule` (was `--gate`), and the lab memory values `basis` and `pitfall` (were `evidence` and `gotcha`). Old values in existing records are still read and are reported with the new word. <!-- legacy -->
- Two adopted skills from K-Dense-AI/science-superpowers (MIT): `investigating-anomalous-results` and `establishing-feasibility-first`, each with a lab addendum, reworded in full into the terminology standard with every rule kept, its license and a `PROVENANCE.md`, and each checked by `tests/claude-code/test-vendored-skills.sh`.
- `science-ultrapowers:preregistering-the-protocol` and `preregister.py`: `register` commits the protocol and then a time-stamp commit with the two trailers, and `check` reports five items (`registered`, `timestamp`, `unaltered`, `chronology`, `inputs`) as clear, not clear or unprovable. The skill carries the binding rule `NO CONFIRMATORY CLAIM WITHOUT A REGISTERED PREDICTION FIRST`. Tests: `skills/preregistering-the-protocol/scripts/tests/test_preregister.py`.
- The templates: the hypothesis record gains the question document's fields; the protocol template gains the design-validation lines; the adversarial review and the referee report list ten critical-appraisal domains; and four new templates join them (the protocol-adherence review, the response matrix, the synthesis matrix and the archive manifest). The findings log gains a `Basis` column, the experiment register gains `Input checksums` and a `feasibility` status, and `lab.json` gains `tracks` and `preregistration`.
- `initializing-a-research-lab --track`: `idea`, `literature`, `review`, `writing` or `dissemination` (repeatable) writes a minimal lab for that track and records it in `lab.json`. Every track's lab holds the decision log, the request queue, the work plan, the task report template, the three meeting templates and `.working/`; the writing track also holds the archive manifest template. On a full lab the flag only adds the track.
- The lifecycle in `running-the-lab`: the seven stages carry the nine phases of science-superpowers as their criteria, with the four binding rules (`NO CONFIRMATORY CLAIM WITHOUT A REGISTERED PREDICTION FIRST`, `NO ADJUSTMENT WITHOUT ROOT CAUSE FIRST`, `NO CLAIM WITHOUT FRESH EVIDENCE`, `Nothing is feasible until it has run`), the protocol-adherence review before the rigor review, and the rule that parallel checks are all reported.
- The basis in `recording-findings`: every finding row carries a basis, confirmatory or exploratory, and a finding is confirmatory only when its registration check is clear. The claim rows set what a null result, a model result and the word "reproduces" need.
- The agents' lifecycle duties: ten agents carry the duties of the lifecycle, among them the feasibility and anomaly skills for the Lab & Experiments Lead, the protocol-adherence review for the Data & Evaluation Researcher, the ten critical-appraisal domains for the Scientific Peer Reviewer, the basis column for the Findings & Records Curator, the registration for the Research Project Manager, the response matrix for the Academic Paper Author and the synthesis matrix for the Literature Researcher.
- The session primer's track table: six rows map what the researcher holds to a track and its first skill, and a service track in a folder with no lab starts with init and `--track`.
- The five track skills, each with a pressure test under `docs/science-ultrapowers/evals/`: `framing-a-research-question` (idea, exits at the Question approval), `synthesizing-the-literature` (literature, with `flow_diagram.py` for the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 and Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) diagrams and the test `test_flow_diagram.py`), `reviewing-a-manuscript` (review, exits with a report in `reviews/reports/`), `writing-for-publication` (writing, with a journal shortlist that names its connector) and `presenting-research` (dissemination: slides, posters, graphical abstracts and architecture figures).
- Init's migration framework gains two column additions: `Basis` on `findings/FINDINGS_LOG.md` (existing rows filled with `exploratory`) and `Input checksums` on `experiments/EXPERIMENT_REGISTER.md` (existing rows empty). They also run on a lab that already has the new layout, and a second run plans nothing. No other record is edited. Tests: `test_init_columns.py` and `test_init_track.py`.
- Init refreshes a stale lab section between the markers in the project's instructions file (`CLAUDE.md`, or `.claude/CLAUDE.md` when it is the only one), keeps everything outside the markers and never writes a second section. A file that is not UTF-8 is left byte for byte, with a warning. The section's second line says that init regenerates the text between the markers.
- A re-run of init keeps the name and short name that `lab.json` records; `--name` is needed only when `lab.json` records none.
- Init's migration: the decision log header becomes `| DEC-id (decision) | Date | Stage | Context | Options | Decision | Rationale | Evidence | Approver |` (Date and Stage are added after the id), and the request queue keeps its 0.1 column order with the new names `| REQ-id (request) | Type | DOI or identifier | Citation (from Crossref) | Requested by and date | Needed for | Priority | Status | Delivered file |`. A rename blocked by an open file warns and the run goes on; a re-run finishes it. A table rewrite that fails leaves the file unchanged, with a warning.
- Init refreshes the 0.1 text a lab still holds: a template, a folder guide or the text above a record's table that is still exactly as 0.1 wrote it is replaced with the current text, shown as a `refresh` line in the plan. A file the researcher edited is kept, with a warning, and the table rows are never touched. The check runs on every lab, so a lab migrated by an earlier build is refreshed by a re-run. Found by acceptance transcript 06, whose session notes still carried the 0.1 headings.
- `writing-for-publication` starts the archive manifest first whenever the decision log holds no Reporting approval, as in a lab set up for the writing track alone, with each line the lab cannot fill marked as missing. Found by acceptance transcript 07; the pressure variant failed with the old text and passed twice with the new.
- The two manifests describe the seven stages and the artifact archive, and the `User-Agent` strings of `verify_doi.py` and `search_openalex.py` carry the plugin version. The terminology test now scans the manifests and the release notes, and `test-version-sync.sh` checks the two `User-Agent` strings.
- One criterion marks a lab in the 0.1 layout, in every script and in the session-start hook: any of the ten numbered folders under `research_lab/`. A lab that holds both an old folder and its new name is refused until the researcher merges the two by hand.
- `preregister.py`: the registration commit is found by its trailer, so a copied or renamed protocol works; the working copy is compared through git's filters, so `core.autocrlf` no longer reports `not clear`; and the stamp goes after a YAML frontmatter block.
- The reviewer-finding statuses are `author-responded`, `re-opened` and `closed` (were `claimed`, `unaddressed` and `resolved`); old rows are still read. <!-- legacy -->
- The memory reminder's switch is `SCIENCE_ULTRAPOWERS_NO_MEMORY_REMINDER`; the older `SCIENCE_ULTRAPOWERS_NO_MEMORY_NUDGE` still works.
- A review of an external manuscript uses the stage value `none (an external manuscript)` in its task assignment.
- "DOI" is never expanded in the plugin's text, and the terminology test refuses the expansion.
- `recording-findings` credits K-Dense-AI/science-superpowers for the basis rule and the claim rows, and `LICENSE` names it.
- Breaking changes for a 0.1 user: the Jev automation rule's JSON keys `rule` and `rule_reason` replace `gate` and `gate_reason`; the `provenance` JSON key `findings` of `artifacts.py` replaces `checks`; and the agent ids are renamed (`lab-project-manager`, `methods-researcher`, `experiments-lead`, `findings-curator`, `paper-author`, `scientific-reviewer` and `citation-auditor` became `research-project-manager`, `methods-innovation-researcher`, `lab-experiments-lead`, `findings-records-curator`, `academic-paper-author`, `scientific-peer-reviewer` and `citation-reference-auditor`), and nothing reads an old id. Update any script or note that reads those keys or names those ids. <!-- legacy -->

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-08)

Initial release, built from obra/superpowers v7.0.2 (MIT) as the template.

- Plugin shell and hooks: the template cut to Claude Code, renamed, with both manifests, the session bootstrap, the cross-platform hook runner and the test runner. <!-- legacy -->
- Artifact store: the `saving-artifacts` skill and `artifacts.py` (save, link, search, path, lineage, graph, check, mark-addressed, resolve, lfs-check) for a versioned, linked artifact store. <!-- legacy -->
- Memory store: the `remembering-for-the-lab` skill, `memory-lint.mjs`, and the nudge and post-compaction hooks. <!-- legacy -->
- Lab skills: `running-the-lab`, `holding-lab-meetings`, `verifying-evidence-by-doi`, `recording-findings`, `requesting-reference-access`, `deciding-with-jev`, `searching-the-literature` and `planning-paper-figures`, each with a pressure test.
- Fourteen agents: the research roles, from the lab project manager and the literature researcher to the paper author and the reviewers, with one G3 definition and the Auditor's gates. <!-- legacy -->
- Init: `initializing-a-research-lab` and `init_lab.py`, the project templates, `connectors.json`, `setup_lab_env.ps1` and the opt-in project permission rules.
- Migrate: `migrating-from-claude-science`, the recorded Claude Science schema and `migrate_claude_science.py`, with `PERMISSIONS.md` and `SKILLS.md` output, fixture tests and a dry run on a database copy.
- Vendored skills with overlays: the kept third-party skills, each with a cleaned overlay and `python -B` in the vendored scripts. <!-- legacy -->
- Tests and gates: `tests/claude-code/run-tests.sh` runs every shell, Python and node test, and the no-foreign-harness gate checks the plugin for project-specific strings, other harness names, executable bits and Git LFS. <!-- legacy -->
- Pre-release review: the review findings are closed, the design documents are sanitised, the leak gates scan them, and a private-strings gate checks for owner-specific strings kept out of the repository. <!-- legacy -->
